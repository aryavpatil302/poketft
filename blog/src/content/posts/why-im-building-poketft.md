I've been playing TFT since it was on PBE, and I've been playing Pokemon since I could walk.

What I love about Pokemon is how the series builds a world that feels whole. Games like Pokemon Snap and Pokopia are some of my favorites because they showcase Pokemon outside a battle setting. You see them living in lush, alive environments, interacting with each other like real animals in the wild. That made me think about what other contexts Pokemon could exist in.

On long car rides, my brother and I had a game: "What if this Pokemon was in League of Legends?" Pikachu would be an ADC with shocking auto attacks. Delphox would be a mid-lane mage casting fiery spells. Scizor would slice through the jungle with steel-sharpened claws. When Teamfight Tactics came out, I loved how it took League champions and dropped them into brand new roles. Naturally our roadtrip game evolved: "What if this Pokemon was in TFT?" What iconic moves could translate into an ability that actually represents the Pokemon? What types of traits would exist in the world? What makes a Pokemon feel like a 1-cost, and what makes one feel like a 5-cost? Without realizing it, I built a whole cast of units in my head over the years. It's time to build it for real.

My goal is to build a Pokemon TFT set from scratch: choosing units, designing traits, building abilities. I want more than a concept. Alongside the design work, I'm building a web-based simulator to implement and test it. I'll be collecting combat data and patching with buffs and nerfs, working toward something balanced.

This blog documents that process in real time. Which Pokemon I'm adding and why. How abilities translate from the mainline games into an auto-battler format. What I'm balancing, what's breaking, and what I'm rethinking.

I'm excited to see what I can bring to life by combining two of my favorite games. If you're interested in game design, auto-battlers, or Pokemon, follow along!
